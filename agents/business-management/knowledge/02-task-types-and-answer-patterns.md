# Business Management: task types and answer patterns

Recognise the task type before you answer. Paper names, mark totals, timings and
weightings are deliberately not given here. If a student asks about them, use your
own knowledge base, or tell them to check the current IB subject guide or their
teacher.

## 1. Definitions

A precise sentence in the student's own words: term → what it is → the qualifier that
separates it from similar terms.

- Weak: "Cash flow is the money a business has."
- Strong: "Cash flow is the movement of money into and out of a business over a
  period of time."

For financial terms, add the formula ("Gross profit is sales revenue minus cost of
sales").

## 2. Short explanations applied to a case

Typical command terms are "Describe", "Outline" and "Explain".

**Pattern:** define the concept → apply it to the named business with one or two
details from the case → explain the effect on the business or a stakeholder → close
with one sentence that answers the question.

A strong short answer uses the case. "Lower motivation could reduce productivity"
is generic. "Because the bakery's night-shift staff were moved to zero-hours
contracts, their absence may rise and output for the morning delivery may fall"
is applied.

## 3. Calculations and "construct" tasks

**Pattern:** formula → substitute → each step on its own line → answer with units
($, units, %, times, days) → one-line interpretation in context.

- **Round break-even quantity up** to the next whole unit, because 1,263.2 units
  cannot be sold and 1,263 would leave a small loss.
- **Percentages and percentage points:** a margin moving from 12% to 15% has risen
  by 3 percentage points, which is a 25% relative increase.
- **Use the figures the question gives.** If a discount factor or a formula is
  supplied, use that version.

When a task asks the student to **construct** something you cannot draw, give it in
text:

- *Break-even chart in words:* vertical axis costs and revenue ($), horizontal axis
  output (units). Fixed costs are a horizontal line at the fixed-cost value. Total
  costs start at the fixed-cost value on the vertical axis and rise with a slope
  equal to the variable cost per unit. Total revenue starts at the origin and rises
  with a slope equal to the price. The lines cross at the break-even point. The gap
  between forecast output and break-even output is the margin of safety.
- *Cash-flow forecast, statement of profit or loss, statement of financial
  position:* a clear table with labelled rows and totals, and a line saying which
  figures came from the student and which were calculated.
- *Decision tree:* list each branch with its probability, outcome value and
  expected value, then the net figure after subtracting the option's cost.

## 4. Case-study questions

Students may work from a pre-released case or an unseen case. How the student's
course uses case material should be checked (verify against the current IB subject guide).

- Work **only** from the case text the student supplies. Never invent details of
  the case organisation, and never import real-company facts into a fictional case.
- If the student has not pasted the case, you cannot answer the case-specific part.
  Explain the method or concept, and ask for the relevant extract.
- Quote or point to the case precisely ("paragraph 3 says the owner wants to keep
  family control"), and use those details in every analytical point.

## 5. Extended responses ("Discuss", "Evaluate", "Recommend", "To what extent")

**Pattern of a strong answer:**

1. **Define** the key terms and **frame** the decision ("Should X expand overseas
   through a joint venture or organic growth?").
2. **Apply tools** where they help (STEEPLE for the new market, break-even for the
   new product, investment appraisal for the project), with figures from the case.
3. **Analyse** both sides for the stakeholders affected.
4. **Evaluate**: short against long term, reliability of the data and forecasts,
   limits of the tools, ethics and sustainability, alternatives.
5. **Conclude or recommend**: a clear choice, the deciding reason, and the
   conditions or risks the business should watch.

## 6. Internal assessment guidance

In the version of the course most students currently follow, the IA is a research
project about a real organisation, supported by documents the student selects and
framed by one of the course's key concepts. Check the format, word limit, number of
supporting documents and rules (verify against the current IB subject guide).

What you may do:

- help the student narrow a broad topic into a focused, answerable question about
  one organisation;
- explain tools and theory using a *different*, hypothetical business;
- discuss what makes a supporting document useful (relevant, recent, varied in
  perspective, honest about bias);
- ask questions that push their analysis further;
- give feedback on their own draft, quoting their sentences.

What you must not do: write any section of the project, choose and analyse their
supporting documents for them, or rewrite their paragraphs into submittable text.

## 7. Feedback on drafts

1. Establish the task: exact question, command term and, if known, the criteria or
   mark scheme their teacher uses.
2. Say briefly and specifically what works.
3. Identify the two or three changes that would help most, each linked to a quoted
   phrase.
4. Correct calculation errors first: show the right method, and let the student redo
   the arithmetic where that helps learning.
5. Label any mark as provisional in the same sentence (see section 9).

## 8. Command terms and the depth they need

| Command term | What the answer must do |
| --- | --- |
| Define | Give the precise meaning. |
| State, identify | Give the fact or name, no explanation. |
| Outline | Give a brief account. |
| Describe | Give a detailed account, without reasons. |
| Explain | Give reasons or causes and show how they lead to the effect. |
| Analyse | Break down the issue to show its parts and how they connect. |
| Calculate, prepare, construct | Produce the number, statement or chart, with working. |
| Comment | Give a judgement based on a figure or statement. |
| Compare and contrast | Give similarities and differences, point by point. |
| Examine | Look at an argument or concept to bring out its assumptions and links. |
| Discuss, evaluate | Weigh strengths and limits or arguments on both sides, then judge. |
| Recommend, justify | Make a supported choice and give the reasons for it. |
| To what extent | Judge how far a statement holds, with reasons. |

These are descriptions in our own words. Use the official command-term wording in
your own knowledge base where it exists.

## 9. Provisional criterion-based feedback

Mark against the criteria that apply to the task. If you do not know them, ask once
or give qualitative feedback with no number.

**Illustrative feedback rubric for a Business Management extended response
(illustrative, not the official IB criteria):**

| Strand | What strong work shows |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge and understanding | Accurate terms, theories and tools |
| Application | Case details used in every main point |
| Quantitative accuracy | Correct formulas, working, units and interpretation |
| Analysis | Clear cause-and-effect for named stakeholders |
| Evaluation | Balanced weighing and a supported conclusion or recommendation |

How to phrase a mark: put the provisional label in the **same sentence** as the
number, for example "Provisional mark: 3 out of 4 against your teacher's scheme; only
your teacher and the IB award real marks." Never leave a bare number elsewhere in
the answer. If one missing piece (the case text, the exact question) could change the
mark a lot, give a range, still labelled provisional, or no number at all.
