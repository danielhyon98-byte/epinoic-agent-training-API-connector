# English A: Language and Literature: supplementary knowledge (proposed)

Supplementary knowledge documents for the **English A: Language and Literature**
specialist (slug `english-a-lang-lit`).

## Status

Proposed supplement, not part of the pinned knowledge-base bundle; adding these to
the bundle would change its SHA-256 and requires a separately reviewed bundle
version.

The specialist's real master prompt and knowledge base live in a repository this
work could not read, so nothing here has been checked against them. Every document
says in its front matter that the specialist's own master prompt and knowledge base
win wherever they disagree. If a reviewer finds a conflict, fix this supplement,
not the master prompt.

## How a builder should load them

1. The specialist's master prompt.
2. The specialist's own knowledge base (the pinned bundle).
3. These documents, in file order: `01` to `04`.
4. The shared answering document, `agents/_shared/knowledge/00-answering-functionality-core.md`.

Do not load this README into the model's context. File names and section numbers
are internal: the documents tell the specialist never to mention them to students.

## Writing rules followed

- Original writing. Nothing is copied or closely paraphrased from IB guides,
  glossaries, past papers, markschemes or study guides.
- All adverts, appeals and posts are invented and labelled as such; brand names are
  made up. No in-copyright text is quoted.
- No URLs, no invented citations, no critics' or theorists' quotations.
- Assessment specifics are not stated as fact. Where one is mentioned, it is marked
  inline "verify … against the current IB subject guide" and listed below.
- The rubric in `02` is labelled "illustrative, not the official IB criteria".

## Facts to verify

Check each against the current IB English A: Language and Literature subject guide,
and against the specialist's master prompt and knowledge base.

1. The current names of the course's broad areas of study and its recurring
   concepts (`01`, section 1).
2. Every row of the course-comparison table in `01`, section 2: texts studied in
   each course, that the unseen analysis in this course uses non-literary texts,
   that the comparative essay is on literary works in both courses, and that the
   oral commonly pairs a literary work with a non-literary body of work.
3. The component names students use ("Paper 1", "Paper 2", "IO", "HL essay") and
   whether they match the current guide edition (`02`, introduction).
4. How the unseen-analysis task differs between SL and HL (`02`, section 1).
5. Whether students have the texts with them for the comparative essay (`02`,
   section 2).
6. The oral format: the literary and non-literary pairing, extract length, timing,
   the role of a global issue, and permitted notes (`02`, section 3).
7. The HL coursework essay: HL-only status, choice of text (literary or
   non-literary) and word limit (`02`, section 4).
8. Whether "written tasks" exist in the current guide or only in earlier editions
   (`02`, section 5).
9. Whether the guide's command-term list and meanings match the depth table in
   `02`, section 7 (the table uses original wording, not the IB glossary).
