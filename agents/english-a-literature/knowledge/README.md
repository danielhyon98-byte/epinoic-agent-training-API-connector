# English A: Literature: supplementary knowledge (proposed)

Supplementary knowledge documents for the **English A: Literature** specialist
(slug `english-a-literature`).

## Status

Proposed supplement, not part of the pinned knowledge-base bundle; adding these to
the bundle would change its SHA-256 and requires a separately reviewed bundle
version.

The specialist's real master prompt and knowledge base live in a repository this
work could not read, so nothing here has been checked against them. Every document
says in its front matter that the specialist's own master prompt and knowledge base
win wherever they disagree. If a reviewer finds a conflict, fix this supplement,
not the master prompt.

## How a builder should load them

1. The specialist's master prompt.
2. The specialist's own knowledge base (the pinned bundle).
3. These documents, in file order: `01` to `04`.
4. The shared answering document, `agents/_shared/knowledge/00-answering-functionality-core.md`.

Do not load this README into the model's context. File names and section numbers
are internal: the documents tell the specialist never to mention them to students.

## Writing rules followed

- Original writing. Nothing is copied or closely paraphrased from IB guides,
  glossaries, past papers, markschemes or study guides.
- Literary examples are either invented for these documents (and labelled
  "invented") or short lines from public-domain works.
- No URLs, no invented citations, no critics' quotations.
- Assessment specifics are not stated as fact. Where one is mentioned, it is marked
  inline "verify … against the current IB subject guide" and listed below.
- The rubric in `02` is labelled "illustrative, not the official IB criteria".

## Public-domain lines quoted (check wording against a reliable edition)

The documents allow the specialist to quote these lines, so their exact wording
matters. Each is given as in common modern editions; spelling and punctuation vary.

- Jane Austen, *Pride and Prejudice*, opening sentence (in `01` and example 6 of `04`).
- William Wordsworth, "I wandered lonely as a cloud", first two lines (in `01`).
- William Shakespeare, *Macbeth*, "Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow," (in `01`).
- Wilfred Owen, "Dulce et Decorum Est", first two lines (in example 4 of `04`,
  supplied by the student in the example).

## Facts to verify

Check each against the current IB English A: Literature subject guide, and against
the specialist's master prompt and knowledge base.

1. The reading-list requirements, including the role of works read in translation
   (`01`, section 1).
2. The current names of the course's broad areas of study and its list of
   recurring concepts (`01`, section 1).
3. The component names students use ("Paper 1", "Paper 2", "IO", "HL essay") and
   whether they match the current guide edition (`02`, introduction).
4. How guiding questions are framed in the unseen-analysis exam, and whether
   students must address them (`02`, section 1).
5. The comparative-essay format, including whether students have the texts with
   them in the exam (`02`, section 2).
6. The oral format: number of works, extract length, timing, the role of a global
   issue, and what notes are permitted (`02`, section 3).
7. The HL coursework essay: that it is HL-only, its requirements and its word limit
   (`02`, section 4; `03`, section 2).
8. Whether the guide's command-term list and meanings match the depth table in
   `02`, section 6 (the table uses original wording, not the IB glossary).
9. The wording of the four public-domain lines listed above.
